What are the characteristics of a tonic seizure?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Tonic seizures cause sudden muscle stiffening, most often in the arms, legs, back, or entire body. These episodes typically last less than 20 seconds and can occur during sleep or while awake. Tonic seizures may cause a person to fall, lose awareness briefly, or experience a temporary loss of control over movement. They are a type of generalized seizure and are commonly associated with epilepsy syndromes such as Lennox-Gastaut.

Because tonic seizures can resemble other neurological or muscular conditions, identifying the underlying cause is essential for effective treatment. If you or a loved one has experienced sudden muscle stiffening, unexplained falls, or brief episodes of rigidity, understanding your symptoms early can help you take the right next step. Explanation

Tonic seizures involve an abrupt increase in muscle tone, meaning the muscles suddenly become stiff. Key features include:

Sudden onset: The person may suddenly become rigid and fall if standing. Muscle stiffening: This typically affects the arms, legs, and back, and may cause the person to extend or bend sharply. Short duration: Most tonic seizures last only a few seconds, often under 20. Impaired awareness: Some people may lose consciousness, while others remain aware but cannot respond. Common during sleep: Tonic seizures often occur at night but can happen during the day as well. Injury risk: Sudden stiffening can lead to falls, so safety precautions are important.
