Myoclonic seizures are sudden, brief, involuntary muscle jerks caused by rapid bursts of abnormal electrical activity in the brain. Common triggers include sleep deprivation, stress, flashing lights, alcohol, and certain medications. These seizures often affect the arms, shoulders, or upper body and may occur shortly after waking. While some myoclonic jerks are harmless, recurring episodes can signal an underlying neurological condition such as juvenile myoclonic epilepsy and warrant medical evaluation.

If you've experienced sudden muscle jerks, unexplained twitching, or suspect a seizure, don't wait to find answers. Explanation

Myoclonic seizures involve quick, involuntary muscle jerks that may affect part or all of the body. Common triggers include:

Sleep deprivation: Lack of sleep can increase the likelihood of seizures. Stress or anxiety: High stress levels may provoke seizures. Flashing or bright lights: Photosensitive epilepsy is triggered by visual stimuli like strobe lights or video games. Alcohol or drug use: Excessive alcohol consumption or certain medications may trigger seizures. Fever or illness: Illness can lower the seizure threshold. Sudden movements or startle: Unexpected stimuli may cause jerks in some individuals.
