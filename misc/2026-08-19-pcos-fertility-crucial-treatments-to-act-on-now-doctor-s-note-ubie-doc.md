How does Polycystic Ovary Syndrome affect fertility, and what treatments are available?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common causes of infertility in women, primarily because it disrupts ovulation. The good news: PCOS-related infertility is often treatable. Key options include:

Lifestyle changes: Even modest weight loss, a balanced diet, and regular exercise can restore ovulation.

Medications: Drugs like letrozole, clomiphene, or metformin help stimulate ovulation and regulate hormones.

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a condition that affects a woman's hormone levels, making it harder for her to get pregnant. Women with PCOS may not ovulate regularly, which means their ovaries don't release eggs every month. This can lead to fertility problems. If you're experiencing symptoms that concern you, you can check them with Ubie's free AI symptom checker to better understand what might be happening with your body.

Treatments for PCOS-related infertility include:

Lifestyle Changes: Eating healthy and exercising can help manage PCOS symptoms and improve fertility.

Medications: Doctors may prescribe medications to help with ovulation. These medications can help the ovaries release eggs more regularly.

Surgery: In some cases, a procedure called ovarian drilling might be recommended. This surgery can help improve ovulation.

Each treatment option has its benefits and risks, and what works for one person may not work for another. It's important for those affected to talk to their healthcare provider to find the best treatment plan for them.
