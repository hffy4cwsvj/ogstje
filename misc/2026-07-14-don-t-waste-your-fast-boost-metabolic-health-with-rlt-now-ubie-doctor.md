What Is Red Light Therapy?

Red light therapy involves exposing your body to low-level wavelengths of red or near-infrared light. It's often delivered via LED panels or handheld devices. Here's what you need to know:

Mechanism: RLT penetrates the skin and can stimulate mitochondrial activity, which may lead to improved cellular energy (ATP) production.

Common Uses: Muscle recovery, skin health (e.g., wrinkle reduction, wound healing), and reducing inflammation.

Safety Profile: Generally well-tolerated. Side effects are rare but can include mild eye strain or temporary redness if the device is too strong or used improperly.

Key point: Most RLT devices are non-invasive and painless. Sessions typically last 5–20 minutes, 2–5 times per week.

How Intermittent Fasting Supports Metabolic Health

Intermittent fasting isn't a diet—it's an eating pattern that alternates between periods of eating and fasting. Popular methods include:

16:8 Method: Fast for 16 hours, eat during an 8-hour window (e.g., noon–8 PM).

5:2 Approach: Normal eating 5 days a week, very low-calorie intake (about 500–600 calories) on 2 non-consecutive days.

Alternate-Day Fasting: Alternating days of normal eating and fasting or very low-calorie intake.

Benefits supported by credible research:

Improved insulin sensitivity

Enhanced fat burning and weight management

Cellular cleanup via autophagy (the body's way of clearing out damaged cells)

Potential reduction in markers of inflammation

Remember: Individual responses vary. Always choose a fasting schedule that fits your lifestyle and health status.

Safety and When to Seek Help

Red light therapy and intermittent fasting are generally safe for healthy adults, but certain groups should be cautious:

Pregnant or breastfeeding women

People with photosensitivity or taking light-sensitive medications

Individuals with diabetes or blood sugar regulation issues

Those with a history of eating disorders

If you have any significant medical conditions or experience concerning symptoms—such as severe dizziness, chest pain, or drastic blood sugar swings—stop on-your-own protocols and speak to a healthcare professional right away.
