# Straighten Up, Fly Right

About 15 years ago, a journalist described me as resembling "a hunchback on stilts." The phrase stuck with me. If I'd thought he was being mean, I'd probably have ignored it, but I suspected he was just being honest. I'm a 6'5" ectomorph (skinny, little muscle mass). And your very tall ectomorphs aren't particularly noted for their brilliant posture. Part of the problem, for me, was the result of having unconsciously tried to look shorter in high school, the better to fit in. Part of it was a generally nonathletic bent (no pun intended), and part of it was about literally not knowing how to stand up straight--not knowing where all the various parts went. I look less like that now, although I'm still unlikely to win any contests for posture. But I know how to stand up, and I feel a lot better. The difference? A decade of Pilates. We're all creatures of habit. I wish I had discovered, when I was younger, that it's possible to cultivate benign habits--to harness the pleasure of compulsive repetition to good ends. Pilates taught me that. Fortunately, I didn't have to decide to do Pilates. My wife decided for me. She was doing Pilates herself, loved it, and insisted I try it. She said it was like yoga, but with spring-loaded furniture. I'd gone to a few yoga classes to appease her, years before, so I thought I knew the drill. I went to my first Pilates class just to get it out of the way. She hadn't been joking about the furniture. Pilates equipment is so weird looking, so 1950s Popular Mechanics, that my curiosity kept me turning up for class. Soon my wife and I bought a Pilates "bed" for ourselves, to use at home. There I learned the first lesson about building a good habit: the need for constant reinforcement. We soon discovered that neither of us tended to use our new equipment much. And when we did, the results were much less satisfactory than when we did the same exercises in the studio. You need a coach for Pilates, a trained instructor. Your body will find the easiest way to perform a given task, and then you won't really be doing Pilates. You will be doing, as one instructor told me, "Bill's Pilates." We sold the spring-loaded bed and booked more studio time. I'd always been too intimidated to join a gym full of beefy gym guys. With Pilates, I had the opposite situation: no arena of male egos, complete absence of machismo and competition. Great. But now I was working out alongside ballet dancers. Slender young women with long, diamond facets of muscle, who do Pilates with inhuman grace and precision. The dancers' presence taught me the second lesson: Concentrate on yourself. Ballet dancers don't regard civilians as competition. They're not competing; they're concentrating. You should be, too. Pilates helps with that: It even matters where you look--the alignment of your eyes--and most of the time I was looking at the ceiling. Having a better idea of how to stand, and having a stronger core to stand with, became by-products of that concentration. But the biggest by-product turned out to be a much greater capacity for relaxation. I hadn't even understood the extent to which that was a problem for me. I worked up to going twice a week, which is probably the minimum for seeing results. Now I go three times a week, every week. I'm not good at keeping up with it when I travel, or over holidays, and I feel it when I return. It takes me a while to get back into the routine after a break, and I don't enjoy it as much. But then "muscle memory," as the instructors say, kicks back in. Which became the third lesson: A good habit is complexly self-reinforcing; it keeps you on track. If you just keep turning up and doing it, there's a mysterious way in which momentum takes over the process. Advertisers always promise big, fast results. What they never tell us is that healthy results are gradual. If you stick with something that you weren't doing before, that is change, right there. Then it changes you more. The habit is the change. Just do it, and then turn up for it again. Repeat! Read on for suggestions on how to stick to your good habits...
