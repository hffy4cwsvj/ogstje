Can antidepressants cause dependence?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Antidepressants are not addictive and do not cause addiction in the way that substances like alcohol or opioids do. However, some people may experience withdrawal-like symptoms—known as antidepressant discontinuation syndrome—when stopping them, especially if stopped abruptly. Symptoms can include dizziness, nausea, fatigue, flu-like feelings, or mood changes. To reduce these effects, doctors typically recommend tapering the dose gradually rather than quitting suddenly.

Explanation

Antidepressants are medications used to treat depression and other mental health conditions. According to the references provided, antidepressants are not considered drugs of abuse or dependence. This means they do not cause addiction in the way substances like alcohol or nicotine do. However, some people may experience withdrawal symptoms if they suddenly stop taking them.

Withdrawal symptoms can include dizziness, headaches, and mood changes. These symptoms are not the same as addiction but can make it challenging to stop the medication suddenly.

If someone is thinking about stopping their antidepressant medication, it is important to talk to a healthcare provider. They can help create a plan to gradually reduce the dose, which can help minimize withdrawal symptoms.
