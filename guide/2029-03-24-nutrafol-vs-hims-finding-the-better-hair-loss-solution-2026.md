Most people experience hair loss at some point in their lives. The most common form of hair loss is called androgenetic alopecia, and it affects something like 50 million men and 30 million women in the United States alone.1

For many people, their hair is an extension of their personality and is thus deeply tied to their self-perception. It’s understandable, then, why someone with androgenetic alopecia would want to take measures to restore their locks to their original, voluminous glory.

Nutrafol and Hims are two companies that aim to help in that regard. One is a supplement company, the other a telehealth platform with various prescription options, and hair loss products are where they intersect. In this review, we assess each company's ability to treat hair loss and determine which one ought to satisfy most people’s needs.

Our bottom line

If you’re in a hurry, know that Hims is our overall recommendation here, as we feel that its prescription-strength ingredients are best suited for regrowing hair lost to androgenetic alopecia.

Hims’ treatments have been more thoroughly analyzed for effectiveness and safety. As long as you can make the up-front financial commitment, they’re more affordable than Nutrafol, too.

The key active ingredients found at Hims — minoxidil and finasteride — have individually gone through multiple stages of clinical testing, received FDA approval, and been subject to ongoing post-market monitoring. It’s accurate to say that they’re more reliable for stopping hair loss and keeping hair. Unless you want only topical minoxidil, you’ll need to access these treatments directly from Hims.

To be sure, Nutrafol offers some of the best hair loss supplements, and research broadly supports their potential to help maintain healthy hair and support regrowth. But we need to be clear that no hair loss supplement has the same research-based substantiation as prescription-strength treatments like minoxidil and finasteride, so their effects are harder to predict. Not to mention that the ingredients often used in supplemental formulations can interact with certain medications or may not agree with individual health histories.

That said, supplemental approaches have a definite place in people's hair recovery journeys, particularly for those who can't take hair loss prescriptions or prefer a nonprescription alternative. And if you're among the subset population for whom supplements are contraindicated, there are other options at your disposal, such as low-level laser therapy (laser caps for hair loss) and hair transplantation.

Effectiveness for hair loss

Remember what we said about supplements versus prescriptions? Even though both options treat androgenetic alopecia via the same biological mechanism, prescriptions have much more scientific support to validate their effectiveness for restoring hair in the highest possible proportion of people. That isn’t to say there’s no scientific support for supplemental ingredients, only that the magnitude of research favors the prescription route.

Hims’ superiority in this field extends to its nonprescription catalog as well, at least in a head-to-head of counterpart products. Take shampoos, for example. While Nutrafol’s shampoo has the makings of a premium hair care product, it lacks much of anything that can treat hair loss, whereas Hims’ Thick Fix Thickening Shampoo features saw palmetto, a botanical that has demonstrated the potential to reduce shedding and promote hair growth whether it’s used orally or topically.2 Had Nutrafol included saw palmetto in its shampoo, as it does in almost all of its oral nutraceuticals, we might have had a more neck-and-neck comparison here.

But solely within the narrow scope of oral supplements, we do think Nutrafol’s nutraceuticals clearly outperform Hims’ biotin gummies. Unless a rare biotin deficiency is contributing to your hair loss, the dietarily abundant B vitamin is unlikely to help.3

Safety

Advantage: Nutrafol

Both brands pose risks of side effects, but the odds of experiencing serious adverse events are significantly lower with Nutrafol.

In Nutrafol, the most likely ingredients to cause problems are marine collagen and saw palmetto — the former for its potential to cause anaphylaxis in allergic populations and the latter because of associated headache and gastrointestinal symptoms.27 8

But these are minor compared to the side effects you could experience with Hims’ minoxidil or finasteride:

In a 2021 multicenter study, oral minoxidil caused numerous systemic adverse events in significant portions of the subject population: lightheadedness (1.7%), fluid retention (1.3%), tachycardia (0.9%), headache (0.4%), periorbital edema (0.3%), and insomnia (0.2%).28

For some men, oral finasteride can cause erectile dysfunction (ED) that persists even after discontinuation (a real danger to a man’s psychological well-being).4 5 There’s also a chance it can cause depression and suicidal thoughts,29 which is a common subject of counseling in patients interested in taking finasteride.

Although Hims’ main hair loss products are subject to greater regulatory oversight because they’re prescriptions,6 the magnitude of their adverse effects is much higher than those of Nutrafol.

Also, as noted by Dr. Scott Bukoski, dermatologist and member of the Innerbody Research Medical Review Board:

“While it is correct that Nutrafol's ingredients haven't gone through the rigor and FDA approval of the prescription medications of Hims, the likely reason for that is that the ingredients are generally safer than the prescription-strength medications and thus do not require that level of scrutiny.”

Cost

In terms of the monthly cost breakdown, you’ll pay less for any one of Hims’ prescription treatments than you would for one of Nutrafol’s main supplements. The most expensive non-bundle option in the Hims prescription catalog is $33 per month for Rx Hair Loss Spray + Ketoconazole + Biotin, which contains both minoxidil and finasteride. That’s approximately half the cost of Nutrafol’s $79 nutraceutical on a subscription plan and about 40% the cost of a one-time purchase ($88 plus $6.95 shipping).

Note, however, that when we talk about “per month” pricing with Hims’ prescriptions, we’re referring to equivalent costs, as there’s often no true month-to-month, pay-as-you-go option. The $33 price point for the Rx Hair Loss Spray + Ketoconazole + Biotin is the cost breakdown for a 300-day supply. The 90- and 150-day plans are less cost-effective, at $59 and $39 per month, respectively.

In other words, Hims requires a larger up-front commitment, but seeing as you need to keep using hair loss treatments to maintain results, we think the pricing structure is reasonable. At any rate, paying up front for a whole 90-, 150-, or 300-day supply of a Hims prescription is still less expensive than 2–5 months of a Nutrafol subscription.

What if we focus exclusively on Hims’ nonprescription offerings? Even then, Hims wins out. Its shampoos and conditioners cost $18–$22, whereas Nutrafol’s counterpart is $44. If that weren’t all, Hims offers bundles that shave off around 10%. Nutrafol has bundles, too, but they don’t seem to yield any savings.

Nutrafol vs. Hims for hair loss

Hair loss treatment is where Nutrafol’s and Hims’ separate business focuses intersect. Nutrafol’s treatments are limited to supplements and hair care products, whereas Hims offers prescription treatments in addition to supplements and care products.

Let’s more closely examine how each company treats hair loss.

Nutrafol for hair loss

Nutrafol’s marquee products are a line of once-daily oral nutraceuticals that deliver different blends of vitamins and botanicals that can potentially reverse hair loss:

Nutrafol Men: A formulation targeted at men’s hair loss

Nutrafol Men 50+: Similar to Nutrafol Men, but with additional ingredients targeting common hair loss factors in advanced age, such as hormones and metabolism

Nutrafol Women: For women aged 18–44 with thinning hair

Nutrafol Postpartum: For women with thinning hair in the first year after giving birth

Elsewhere in the catalog are various support products that may help reinforce the benefits provided by the nutraceuticals: shampoos, conditioners, serums, and a scalp mask. The shampoos, conditioners, and scalp mask are unremarkable and appear to be formulated more toward general hair health, but the serums have some promising ingredients.

Speaking of, here are what we consider to be the primo ingredients used in Nutrafol’s best products:

Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha has stress-relieving properties that may help with androgenetic alopecia, as stress can accelerate the hair cycle and prompt the body to attack hair follicles, leading to increased hair loss.9

Ashwagandha features in all of Nutrafol’s nutraceuticals except the Postpartum formula, as it isn’t suitable for breastfeeding mothers.10 It’s also in both the women’s and men’s hair serums.

Saw palmetto

Saw palmetto blocks the uptake of a testosterone derivative called dihydrotestosterone (DHT).8 DHT is an androgen, or a sex hormone, and one of the main drivers of androgenetic alopecia. In addition to being genetically predisposed to responding excessively to androgens, people with androgenetic alopecia tend to have higher levels of DHT and more androgen receptors in the scalp, so it follows that reducing total DHT in the body can potentially reverse hair loss.11

The Postpartum formula is the only Nutrafol nutraceutical that doesn’t use saw palmetto. Like ashwagandha, it’s not suitable for breastfeeding mothers.12

Curcumin

Curcumin is a compound in turmeric that's rich in polyphenols, which have anti-inflammatory properties.13 Also, when taken alongside zinc, curcumin can protect the scalp against oxidative stress (a hair loss factor) while inhibiting DHT.14 15

Curcumin is absent in the Postpartum nutraceutical because it can suppress milk production.16

Hyaluronic acid

Like curcumin, hyaluronic acid has a protective effect against oxidative stress.17 It’s excluded from the Women’s Balance and Postpartum formulas, though the reasoning is unclear.

Moldavian dragonhead

[the Better](https://musclemend.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/8407311555.html)

Moldavian dragonhead is an herbaceous plant that supports collagen production, which in turn may prolong the growth phase of the human hair cycle.30 31 In the Nutrafol catalog, it appears only in Nutrafol Men 50+.

Spermidine

Spermidine is a polyamine best known as a potent antioxidant, but it has also been found to stimulate hair growth.32 It, too, appears only in Nutrafol Men 50+.

Pea sprout extract

Pea sprout extract, the key component in Nutrafol Women Postpartum, was shown in a 2020 study to improve hair density and reduce hair loss.18

We wish we could specify how much of each ingredient is in every four-capsule serving of a Nutrafol nutraceutical, but dosages are hidden behind proprietary blends. That limits how much we can say about Nutrafol’s efficacy, and it’s a small part of why we consider Hims to be the better brand for hair loss.

Hims’ hair loss treatments include prescription medications as well as supplements and hair care products.

Which one is safer, Nutrafol or Hims?

Nutrafol is generally the safer option because its ingredients pose less risk of serious side effects than Hims’ prescription-strength counterparts. People who aren’t allergic to fish collagen (a key ingredient in several Nutrafol products) should have little to no trouble with Nutrafol’s supplements. Some other ingredients can be unsafe for lactating mothers,10 12 16 and the saw palmetto can cause headaches and gastrointestinal symptoms,8 but those are minor compared to the health concerns associated with finasteride and minoxidil:

Oral finasteride has been associated with a 2–5% incidence of sexual side effects (e.g., ED) that may persist even after discontinuation of use.26 4 This is because finasteride prevents the conversion of testosterone into DHT, and DHT is necessary for normal sexual function.

Oral finasteride has also been linked to depression and suicidal ideation.29 It isn’t uncommon for patients to undergo psychological counseling before starting oral finasteride treatment.

Oral minoxidil is known to cause numerous systemic adverse events, such as lightheadedness, fluid retention, tachycardia, headache, periorbital edema, and insomnia.28

Having said that, we ought to acknowledge that Hims’ main hair loss products, being prescriptions, have undergone a series of rigors on their developmental path. In a nutshell, the drug development life cycle involves multiple phases of clinical testing. Safety is the focus of the first phase, and it remains a key consideration as a drug advances to phases two and three. Later, it must be approved by the FDA before it hits the market, and once on the market, it undergoes active safety monitoring.23 24 Minoxidil and finasteride have withstood this crucible, and they continue to do so.

Nevertheless, it’s hard to overlook the magnitude of side effects with Hims’ prescription-strength treatments, especially when compared to Nutrafol.

Cost comparison: Nutrafol vs. Hims

Although Hims’ catalog centers mostly on prescription drugs, its hair loss prescriptions are still generally less expensive than any parallel product in the Nutrafol catalog.

Outside of bundles, Hims’ most expensive hair loss treatment is the Rx Hair Loss Spray + Ketoconazole + Biotin, which starts at an equivalent of $59 per month for the 90-day supply, $39 per month for the 150-day supply, and $33 per month for the 300-day supply. Even at its least cost-effective, it’s around $20–$30 less than a Nutrafol nutraceutical.

Other Hims prescription options range from $15 to $29 per month for the most popular purchase plans, and the supplemental/hair care treatments are even more affordable at around $16–$22.

As for Nutrafol, its marquee nutraceuticals are each $79 on subscription and $88 as one-time purchases. The other items range from $44 for a shampoo or conditioner to $49 for a serum or scalp mask.

The most affordable Nutrafol products are the Hair Wellness Boosters, which can be had for as little as $10. But they don’t really count since they’re sold as add-ons to the nutraceuticals, not stand-alone items.

Clearly, the pricing analysis deeply favors Hims. Even though it requires a relatively high up-front payment for a multi-month commitment (recall, it doesn’t offer true month-to-month pricing), you’ll have paid less after 2–5 months than you would have with Nutrafol, even with the $5 processing fee that Hims adds to all orders.

Refund policies

Nutrafol has a better refund policy than Hims only because Nutrafol actually has a refund policy delineated on its website. It states that you can return any unopened item within 30 days, with free return shipping in the United States. No, it’s not a good policy, seeing as you can’t even use the product to see if you like it, but at least it exists.

Hims doesn’t have a set policy at all, or not a general one. Officially, the company states that “the nature of [its] products” means it can’t accommodate returns. The one possible exception is if you have an active subscription but decide to cancel a delivery before the pharmacy processes your order. In that case, you’ll have to reach out to customer support to work out an arrangement, which isn’t guaranteed.

Which company has better customer support?

Nutrafol wins in the customer support arena for its responsiveness and ease of access. You can activate chat support by clicking the green button located in the lower right-hand corner of every page. At first, the AI assistant will urge you to consult the knowledge hub, but entering “agent” ought to connect you with a live representative. In our most recent experience with Nutrafol’s chat function, we connected with a rep within a minute and received a satisfactory answer in less than two. The company’s email support may still take several days to a week to respond, but the chat function is top-shelf.

Hims offers excellent chat support, too, but it’s available only to existing customers, not prospective ones. If you aren’t yet a patient but have an important question, you must submit it through Hims’ contact page.

The thing about Hims’ contact page is that it’s a bit hard to find. Normally, companies have a link on the home page that includes the word “contact,” but Hims doesn’t. Instead, it has a link labeled “Customer Help Center” that leads to a page with a link that leads to another page with the contact. It’s the sort of excessive layering that stems from either the company’s overindulgent website design or, more cynically, its desire to avoid customer interactions.

Final thoughts

Hims’ and Nutrafol’s primary hair loss treatments have common ground in that they both seek to minimize systemic DHT. They diverge in that Hims’ prescription options have a greater magnitude of scientific research to support their use as effective solutions for androgenetic alopecia. Yet Nutrafol certainly has its place as an alternative to prescription drugs or perhaps a helpful addition alongside more powerful treatments. To determine the best option for you, we recommend discussing your goals with your healthcare provider. Together, you can identify a drug or supplement that presents the best odds of restoring your hair.
