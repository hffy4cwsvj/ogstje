Chronic Widespread Pain: Differentiating Two Common Conditions

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Chronic widespread pain is most commonly linked to two conditions: fibromyalgia and peripheral neuropathy.

Fibromyalgia causes a diffuse, dull ache with tender points, persistent fatigue, disrupted sleep, and cognitive difficulties often called "fibro fog."

Peripheral neuropathy produces burning or electric shock–like sensations, numbness, and weakness that follow specific nerve pathways.

These conditions differ significantly in their causes, diagnostic criteria, and treatments — so identifying which one you may be experiencing is a critical first step. Because symptoms often overlap with other conditions, self-diagnosis can be misleading and delay effective care.

Explanation

Chronic widespread pain affects millions of people, making daily activities a challenge. Two frequently discussed causes are fibromyalgia and neuropathy. Although they share some symptoms, understanding their differences helps you seek the right treatment and improve your quality of life.

What Is Fibromyalgia?

Fibromyalgia is a long-term condition characterized by widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, and tenderness in localized areas.

Key features of fibromyalgia:

Pain distribution: Often described as a constant, dull ache affecting both sides of the body, above and below the waist.

Tender points: Specific spots on the neck, shoulders, chest, hips, knees, and elbows that hurt when pressed.

Fatigue and cognitive issues: Commonly called "fibro fog," this includes memory lapses and difficulty concentrating.

What causes fibromyalgia?

The precise cause is unknown, but it likely involves abnormal pain processing in the central nervous system.

Diagnosis

Based on a thorough history and physical exam.

American College of Rheumatology criteria focus on pain locations (widespread pain index) and severity of symptoms (symptom severity scale).

No specific blood test confirms fibromyalgia; tests mainly rule out other conditions.

Management

Medications: Low-dose antidepressants (e.g., amitriptyline), certain anticonvulsants (e.g., pregabalin), and pain relievers.

Lifestyle changes: Regular low-impact exercise (walking, swimming), good sleep hygiene, and stress reduction.

Therapies: Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), physical therapy, and mindfulness practices.

Self-care: Gentle stretching, pacing activities, and heat or cold therapy.

What Is Neuropathy?

Neuropathy (peripheral neuropathy) refers to damage or dysfunction of peripheral nerves, leading to pain, numbness, and weakness, usually in the hands and feet.

Key features of neuropathy:

Pain type: Sharp, burning, or electric shock–like sensations.

Sensory changes: Numbness, tingling ("pins and needles"), or heightened sensitivity to touch.

Motor symptoms: Muscle weakness, cramping, and balance problems.

Autonomic involvement (in some cases): Dizziness when standing, digestive issues, or changes in sweating.

Common causes of neuropathy:

Diabetes: The most common cause in developed countries.

Chemotherapy: Certain cancer treatments can damage nerves.

Vitamin deficiencies: Particularly B12 deficiency.

Autoimmune diseases: Such as rheumatoid arthritis or lupus.

Infections: Lyme disease, HIV, or shingles (herpes zoster).

Toxins and medications: Heavy metals, alcohol abuse, and some antibiotics.

Clinical exam: Tests for reflexes, strength, and sensation.

Nerve conduction studies: Measure how fast electrical signals move through nerves.

Electromyography (EMG): Evaluates muscle response to nerve stimulation.

Blood tests: Check glucose levels, vitamin B12, thyroid function, and markers of autoimmune disease.

Treat underlying cause: Control blood sugar in diabetes, correct nutritional deficiencies.

Medications: Antidepressants (duloxetine), anticonvulsants (gabapentin), topical treatments (capsaicin), and pain relievers.

Physical therapy: Strength exercises and balance training.

Lifestyle adjustments: Safe footwear, fall prevention, and gentle, regular exercise.

Overlapping Symptoms and Key Differences

Since fibromyalgia and neuropathy can both involve chronic pain, it's easy to confuse the two. Here's how to tell them apart:

Pain pattern• Fibromyalgia: Diffuse, affecting muscles and soft tissues.• Neuropathy: Follows the path of specific nerves, often in extremities.

Sensory signs• Fibromyalgia: Tender points, but typically no true numbness.• Neuropathy: Numbness, tingling, burning, or electric-shock sensations.

Muscle strength• Fibromyalgia: Strength usually remains intact.• Neuropathy: May cause muscle weakness or loss.

Associated issues• Fibromyalgia: Cognitive problems, widespread fatigue.• Neuropathy: Autonomic symptoms (dizziness, heart-rate changes), more focal neurological deficits.

When to Seek Medical Advice

Both conditions benefit from early evaluation. Make an appointment if you notice:

Sudden or severe weakness in arms or legs

Loss of bladder or bowel control

Unexplained weight loss or fever

Severe, unrelenting pain that doesn't improve with self-care

New numbness or tingling that spreads rapidly

Diagnostic Approach

A healthcare provider will typically:

Take a detailed history

Perform a physical and neurological exam

Order laboratory tests (e.g., blood sugar, B12 level)

Recommend nerve studies if neuropathy is suspected

Use clinical criteria for fibromyalgia (tender points, widespread pain index)

Remember, no single test definitively diagnoses fibromyalgia. Neuropathy often shows up on nerve conduction studies or EMG.

Treatment Strategies

Both conditions require a tailored, multidimensional approach.

Fibromyalgia management:

Start with low-dose medications targeting pain and sleep

Practice stress-management techniques

Consider CBT or supportive counseling

Neuropathy management:

Treat the root cause (e.g., improve blood sugar control)

Use medications specifically for nerve pain

Protect feet and hands from injury if sensation is reduced

Engage in physical therapy to maintain strength and balance

Lifestyle and Self-Care Tips

No matter the diagnosis, these habits support long-term well-being:

Balanced exercise: Aim for low-impact activities like walking, swimming, or yoga.

Healthy sleep: Keep a regular schedule, create a calm bedroom environment.

Stress reduction: Try meditation, deep breathing, or gentle hobbies.

Nutrition: Eat a varied diet rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and healthy fats.

Support network: Join a support group or talk to trusted friends and family.

Talk to a Doctor

Only a qualified healthcare professional can diagnose and manage fibromyalgia or neuropathy. If you experience any serious or life-threatening symptoms—such as sudden weakness, trouble breathing, or loss of bladder/bowel control—seek emergency care immediately. For non-urgent but concerning symptoms, schedule an appointment to discuss your pain and get personalized guidance.

Chronic widespread pain can feel overwhelming, but understanding whether it's fibromyalgia or neuropathy is a crucial step toward relief. With the right diagnosis, treatments, and self-care strategies, you can take control of your health and improve your daily functioning. If you have any doubts or your condition worsens, please speak to a doctor right away.
