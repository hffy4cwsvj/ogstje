Eliquis Side Effects: Why Your Body Is Reacting + Medically Approved Next Steps

If you've been prescribed Eliquis (apixaban), you were likely told it helps prevent dangerous blood clots. And that's true. Eliquis is a direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) used to reduce the risk of stroke in atrial fibrillation, treat deep vein thrombosis (DVT), prevent pulmonary embolism (PE), and reduce clot recurrence.

But like all medications that affect blood clotting, Eliquis side effects can happen. Some are mild. Others require urgent medical attention. Understanding why your body is reacting can help you stay safe and make informed decisions.

Let's break it down clearly and calmly.

Why Eliquis Causes Side Effects

Eliquis works by blocking Factor Xa, an essential protein in your clotting system. This slows your blood's ability to clot.

That's the goal — but it also explains most side effects.

When clotting is reduced:

You bleed more easily

Bleeding may last longer

Small injuries can produce larger bruises

Internal bleeding risks increase

Most Eliquis side effects are related to this mechanism.

Common Eliquis Side Effects

These are typically mild but should still be monitored:

1. Easy Bruising

You may notice bruises appearing more easily than before, sometimes without remembering an injury.

Why it happens: Small blood vessels leak slightly under the skin and take longer to clot.

2. Minor Bleeding

This can include:

Nosebleeds

Bleeding gums

Heavier menstrual bleeding

Small cuts that bleed longer than usual

These are expected in some patients and not automatically dangerous — but persistent bleeding should be discussed with a doctor.

3. Mild Anemia

Slow, unnoticed blood loss can lead to:

Fatigue

Weakness

Shortness of breath

Pale skin

Your doctor may monitor blood counts during treatment.

More Serious Eliquis Side Effects

While less common, these require prompt medical attention.

1. Internal Bleeding

This is the most serious risk with Eliquis.

Signs may include:

Red, dark, or tarry stools

Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds

Coughing up blood

Severe headache

Who Is at Higher Risk for Eliquis Side Effects?

Certain factors increase bleeding risk:

Age over 75

Kidney disease

Liver disease

History of gastrointestinal bleeding

Previous stroke

Use of NSAIDs (like ibuprofen or naproxen)

Use of aspirin or other blood thinners

Heavy alcohol use

Low body weight

Your doctor weighs these risks carefully before prescribing Eliquis. For many patients, the benefit of preventing a stroke outweighs the bleeding risk.

Why You Should Not Stop Eliquis Suddenly

One of the most important safety points:

Do not stop Eliquis without medical supervision.

Stopping suddenly can sharply increase your risk of:

Stroke

Deep vein thrombosis

Pulmonary embolism

If side effects occur, your doctor may:

Adjust the dose

Switch medications

Investigate underlying causes

Medically Approved Next Steps If You Notice Side Effects

Here's a practical, safe approach.

Step 1: Assess Severity

Ask yourself:

Is the bleeding mild or heavy?

Is it stopping on its own?

Do I feel dizzy, faint, or weak?

Is there severe pain?

Mild bruising or minor bleeding → Call your doctor during office hours.

Heavy bleeding or stroke symptoms → Seek emergency care immediately.

Step 2: Contact Your Doctor Promptly

Call your prescribing doctor if you experience:

Repeated nosebleeds

Blood in urine

Blood in stool

Significant fatigue

Large unexplained bruises

Ongoing gum bleeding

Severe menstrual bleeding

Your doctor may order:

Blood tests

Kidney function tests

Imaging studies (if internal bleeding is suspected)

Step 3: Reduce Preventable Risks

You can lower your risk of Eliquis side effects by:

Avoiding NSAIDs unless approved

Limiting alcohol

Using a soft toothbrush

Shaving with an electric razor

Being cautious to prevent falls

Informing all healthcare providers you take Eliquis

Wearing medical alert identification

Step 4: Monitor Your Body Carefully

Keep track of:

New symptoms

Pattern of bruising

Bowel movement color

Energy levels

Any changes after starting new medications

Bring this information to your doctor. It helps guide safe treatment decisions.

Heavy or unstoppable bleeding

Frequently Asked Questions About Eliquis Side Effects

How long do side effects last?

Minor bruising may continue throughout treatment. More serious side effects require medical evaluation.

Does Eliquis damage organs?

It does not directly damage organs, but bleeding can affect organs if severe and untreated.

Can diet affect Eliquis?

Unlike warfarin, Eliquis is not significantly affected by vitamin K intake. However, alcohol and certain medications can increase bleeding risk.

Is there an antidote?

Yes. A reversal agent (andexanet alfa) exists for life-threatening bleeding in hospital settings.

The Bottom Line

Eliquis side effects are primarily related to bleeding. Most are manageable, but some can be serious.

Never stop Eliquis without speaking to a doctor.
