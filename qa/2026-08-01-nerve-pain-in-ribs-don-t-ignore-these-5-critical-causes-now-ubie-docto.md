Nerve pain in the ribs, known as intercostal neuralgia, happens when the nerves running between or near the ribs become irritated, compressed, or inflamed. Common causes include shingles, rib injuries, slipping rib syndrome, post-surgical nerve damage, or spinal issues. Symptoms often include sharp, burning, or stabbing pain along the rib cage that may worsen with breathing, coughing, or movement.

Because rib nerve pain can mimic more serious conditions—such as heart, lung, or gallbladder problems—identifying the true cause matters. Nerve pain in the ribs is commonly associated with a condition known as intercostal neuralgia. This happens when the nerves located between your ribs become irritated, stretched, or compressed. These nerves can be affected by a variety of factors such as trauma, repetitive strain, or even inflammation. Slipping rib syndrome, for example, occurs when one or more of the ribs move in an unusual way, which can pinch or irritate the nearby nerves, leading to sharp or burning pain. Additionally, certain anatomical variations like the presence of a cervical rib can also cause nerve compression, contributing to the pain that may be felt along the rib cage. Other causes might include nerve damage from infections or inflammation in the surrounding muscles and tissues. Each of these scenarios can lead to nerve pain that feels different from other types of chest or rib pain.
