Internal Medicine

Non-nutritive sweeteners, such as aspartame, sucralose, and stevia, are generally recognized as safe by regulatory agencies like the FDA. However, emerging research suggests potential concerns regarding their long-term impact on health. Studies have linked frequent consumption to changes in gut microbiota, altered glucose metabolism, increased cravings, and a possible higher risk of cardiovascular events, including stroke and heart disease. While moderate use is unlikely to cause harm for most people, individual responses can vary based on genetics, existing health conditions, and overall diet.

If you've noticed symptoms like unexplained headaches, digestive issues, blood sugar swings, or heart palpitations, it may be difficult to know whether sweeteners—or something else entirely—are the cause. Explanation

Non-nutritive sweeteners, often used as sugar substitutes, are generally safe for most people. However, there are some concerns about how they might affect health.

Health Outcomes: According to one study, these sweeteners might not significantly impact weight or other health outcomes in the short term. But, there is still some uncertainty about their long-term effects.

Heart Health: Another study suggests that while they are sugar-free, these sweeteners might still pose some risks to heart health. It's important to use them in moderation.

Metabolic Health: Some research indicates that non-nutritive sweeteners could affect metabolism, which is how your body uses energy. This might have implications for weight and heart health.
