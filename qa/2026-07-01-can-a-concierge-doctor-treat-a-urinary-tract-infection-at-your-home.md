A urinary tract infection is an infection that can be found in any part of your urinary system, which includes the kidney, urethra, ureters, and bladder. The most common areas for infections are the urethra and bladder.

What are the causes of UTIs?

The presence of bacteria in the urinary tract is the most common cause of UTIs. The bacteria are typically introduced in the urethra and make their way to the bladder, where they multiply. The urinary tract typically fights any foreign body automatically, but these defenses sometimes don’t work and fail altogether. When the urinary tract is defenseless against bacteria, then it has the potential to multiply and result in an infection.

How is UTI treated?

Since UTI is considered an infection, antibiotics will be the most common treatment plan. The specific type of antibiotics depends on your medical history and the symptoms you feel. It is important to consult with a medical professional if you have a slight inkling that you might have a UTI.

Once you are prescribed antibiotics, it might take a few days to a week to clear. If you are experiencing pain, your doctor will also prescribe an analgesic to help alleviate the pain.
