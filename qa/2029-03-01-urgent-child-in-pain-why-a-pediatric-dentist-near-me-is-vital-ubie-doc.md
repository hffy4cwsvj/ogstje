Common Causes of Tooth Pain in Children

If your child is in pain, a pediatric dentist near me will evaluate for the most common causes:

1. Dental Caries (Cavities)

The most common chronic disease in children. Cavities happen when bacteria break down tooth enamel.

Signs include:

Tooth sensitivity to hot, cold, or sweets

Visible holes or dark spots

Pain when chewing

Bad breath

If you're noticing these symptoms and want to understand whether Dental Caries might be the cause, you can use a free AI-powered symptom checker to get personalized insights and guidance before scheduling your appointment.

2. Dental Abscess (Infection)

An untreated cavity can lead to infection inside the tooth or gum.

Watch for:

Severe, constant pain

Swelling of the face or gums

Fever

Pus or bad taste in the mouth

This requires urgent dental care.

3. Teething (Infants and Toddlers)

Teething can cause:

Irritability

Mild gum swelling

Drooling

Mild temperature elevation

Teething pain is usually mild and temporary.

4. Dental Trauma

Falls and sports injuries are common in children.

Seek immediate evaluation if you notice:

A knocked-out tooth

Cracked or broken tooth

Bleeding that won't stop

Tooth displacement

5. Gum Inflammation (Gingivitis)

Red, swollen, or bleeding gums may signal early gum disease—often due to poor brushing or plaque buildup.

Medically Approved Next Steps If Your Child Has Tooth Pain

If your child complains of dental pain, here's what you should do:

✅ 1. Stay Calm and Assess

Ask simple questions:

Where does it hurt?

When did it start?

Does it hurt when chewing?

Is there swelling?

Look inside the mouth with clean hands and good light.

✅ 2. Rinse With Warm Salt Water

If your child is old enough to spit safely:

Mix ½ teaspoon of salt in 8 ounces of warm water.

Have them gently rinse and spit.

This can reduce inflammation and bacteria temporarily.

Avoid aspirin in children.

Do not place aspirin directly on the tooth or gum.

✅ 4. Avoid Trigger Foods

Until you see a pediatric dentist near me, avoid:

Sugary foods

Ice-cold drinks

Very hot foods

Hard or sticky snacks

✅ 5. Schedule a Pediatric Dental Visit Promptly

When It's an Emergency

Some dental problems require immediate care.

Seek urgent dental or medical attention if your child has:

Facial swelling

Difficulty breathing or swallowing

High fever

Severe, worsening pain

Trauma with uncontrolled bleeding

Preventing Future Dental Pain

After this issue is resolved, prevention becomes the focus.

Daily Habits That Protect Your Child's Teeth

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste

Use a pea-sized amount (smear for under age 3)

Floss once daily once teeth touch

Limit sugary snacks and drinks

Avoid bedtime bottles with milk or juice

Schedule dental visits every 6 months

Fluoride strengthens enamel and significantly reduces cavity risk.

Addressing Dental Anxiety

Many parents search for a pediatric dentist near me because their child is scared. That's normal.

Swelling spreading to the eye or neck

Signs of dehydration
