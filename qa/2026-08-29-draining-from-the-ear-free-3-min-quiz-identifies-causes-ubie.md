About the Symptom

Otorrhea, or ear discharge, is any fluid that comes from the ear. It's usually due to an infection (swimmer's ear or otitis externa) but it can also be just moist wax. Sometimes bad middle ear infections (acute otitis media) can lead to eardrum rupture and drainage as well. Drainage, if chronic, can be indicative of a more serious condition, such as a skin cyst in the ear (cholesteatoma).

Possible Causes

Generally, Draining from the ear can be related to:

Infection of the middle ear (area just behind the eardrum) by bacteria or viruses. Contributing factors include nasal obstruction by adenoids and allergic rhinitis. The symptoms can include ear pain or fullness, fevers, and muffled hearing. This is more common in children but can also present in adults. In severe cases, the eardrum may rupture leading to drainage coming out the ear canal.

Related Serious Diseases

Sometimes, Draining from the ear may be related to these serious diseases:

A hole in the eardrum. It is typically caused by direct injury, but can also be caused by severe infections in the middle ear (otitis media).

Doctor's Diagnostic Questions

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this symptom:
