What are fillers?

Face fillers are cosmetic injections with a gel-like texture. They’re made from FDA-approved natural or synthetic substances. Most are hyaluronic acid-based.

The substance gets injected into the dermis layer of the skin. It aims to correct specific conditions. Fillers may reduce wrinkles and help plump up soft tissue by adding volume to it.

They act like collagen, a naturally occurring protein in the body responsible for keeping your skin supple. Fillers have a similar role in hydrating the skin and making it appear youthful. In some cases, people use these injectables to rebuild the structure of healthy skin.

Types of fillers

Different dermal fillers are suited to specific purposes. Your practitioner will provide you with advice depending on the area you want to treat.

Doing your research before the procedure can give you more peace of mind. Technically, fat grafting also falls into the category of fillers. However, we’ll concentrate on the options that are readily available off the shelf.

Fillers with hyaluronic acid

What are face fillers made of? The most popular option is hyaluronic acid. This substance naturally occurs in your body. It keeps the skin looking volumized and has hydrating properties.

Over time, the body’s production of hyaluronic acid decreases. Natural injectables usually last 6–12 months. In some cases, these fillers can last as long as two years.

Fillers with calcium hydroxylapatite

Like hyaluronic acid, calcium hydroxylapatite (CaHA) occurs naturally in the body. The purpose of this filler differs slightly from the former. It lasts roughly 12–18 months in most cases.

Cosmetic professionals typically administer CaHA injectables for deeper wrinkles. They also work well for smoothing skin on the chest and rejuvenating the appearance of the neck.

Fillers with poly-L-lactic acid

Dermal fillers containing poly-L-lactic acid could help with collagen production. This compound is essential for creating connective tissue.

When injected, it may smooth fine lines, and in some cases, reduce deep wrinkles. The results are relatively long-lasting. Your skin could appear youthful for up to two years.

Fillers with polymethyl methacrylate

One of the longest-lasting dermal fillers is polymethyl methacrylate. It’s made of collagen and minute balls that plump up the skin from within.

It could reduce the visibility of even severe wrinkles. This filler option is mainly popular due to how long it lasts. In some cases, the results are visible for up to five years.

How dermal fillers work

How do face fillers work? A professional administers a gel-like substance into the skin. They use either a needle or cannula for the procedure.

The substance inside is the filler, and it adds volume to the skin from within the dermal layer. It replaces the proteins or fats that are deficient in the area due to age.

What to expect when getting fillers

You might be curious about how things proceed once you arrive to get these injectables. It’s a quick process and done with minimal fuss. Advocates of fillers even call it “the lunch hour lift.”

Start preparing for your procedure approximately seven days beforehand. You’ll receive a detailed list of products to avoid when you make your appointment.

Stop consuming alcohol, blood thinners, and products like fish oils, vitamin E, ibuprofen, aspirin, and turmeric. It’s also essential that you don’t get any dental work done or take vaccines two weeks prior to your appointment.

The procedure starts with the application of a topical cream. Your practitioner administers it to numb the site of the injection. In other instances, the filler already contains a local anesthetic combined. These cases don’t require a numbing agent before the procedure.

The next step is to sterilize the injection site. Thereafter, the filler is administered. You can resume your usual activities shortly after you receive the filler. There’s typically no down-time required after getting the procedure.

Many practitioners advise patients to avoid strenuous activities for 48 hours. Taking it easy for a short period facilitates healing.

There may be some redness, tenderness, or bruising. These side effects usually ease within a few days. Aftercare is essential. Be sure to follow your doctor’s instructions.

Risks and side effects of dermal fillers

This cosmetic procedure sounds like a dream come true for skin care, but are there any negatives? Fortunately, dermal fillers don’t have a long list of risks and side effects.

There are some potential discomforts. But they aren’t as serious as those from more invasive skin care therapies.

Common side effects are redness, bruising, and tenderness. Some patients also report itching sensations. There may be a moderate amount of pain that lasts a few days at most.

In rare cases, sores and tissue infections may occur. However, professionals can assist with proper aftercare if problems occur. In the event of an extreme reaction, antibiotics can be administered.
