Doctor's Note: Zepbound vs Wegovy for Black Women Over 40

As more people turn to GLP-1 receptor agonists (RAs) for weight management and metabolic health, two names stand out: Zepbound (tirzepatide) and Wegovy (semaglutide). Both have proven effective in clinical trials, but how do they compare—especially for Black women over 40, who face unique health considerations? Below, we break down the evidence, highlight special considerations, and help you decide which option might be best for you.

1. Understanding GLP-1 and GIP/GLP-1 RAs

GLP-1 receptor agonists (like semaglutide) mimic the gut hormone GLP-1:

Increase insulin secretion when glucose is high

Slow gastric emptying (promoting fullness)

Reduce appetite and food intake

Dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists (tirzepatide = Zepbound) add GIP receptor activation:

GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) also enhances insulin release

May further improve blood sugar control and weight loss

2. Efficacy: What the Trials Show

Wegovy (Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly)

STEP trials enrolled adults with obesity or overweight plus at least one weight-related condition.

Average weight loss at 68 weeks: 14.9%–17.4% of baseline body weight.

Improvements in blood pressure, A1C (for those with type 2 diabetes), and cardiovascular risk markers.

Zepbound (Tirzepatide up to 15 mg weekly)

SURMOUNT trials focused on obesity without requiring diabetes.

Average weight loss at 72 weeks: 15%–20.9% of baseline body weight (dose-dependent).

Greater reductions in A1C in people with type 2 diabetes (SURPASS trials).

Bottom line on efficacy:

Both drugs achieve substantial weight loss.

Zepbound's dual action may lead to slightly greater weight reduction (especially at higher doses).

Individual response varies widely; some may do better on one vs. the other.

3. Safety and Side Effects

Both medications share common GI side effects, especially during dose escalation:

Key differences:

Wegovy

Gradual titration over 16–20 weeks to 2.4 mg minimizes GI upset.

Rare cases of gallbladder disease; monitor if you have gallstones.

Zepbound

Titration over 20 weeks to 15 mg; GI side effects may be more pronounced at higher doses.

Slightly higher rates of injection-site reactions.

Monitor for signs of pancreatitis (severe abdominal pain).

Both carry a boxed warning about possible thyroid C-cell tumors (based on rodent studies). Human relevance is unclear but use caution if you have a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 syndrome.

4. Why Black Women Over 40 Need Special Consideration

Higher prevalence of hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and obesity

Unique fat distribution patterns (more subcutaneous vs. visceral)

Historical underrepresentation in clinical trials

Cultural, socioeconomic, and access barriers

Studies suggest:

Weight-loss response to GLP-1 RAs is broadly similar across races/ethnicities.

Improved glycemic control can reduce risks of diabetes complications, which disproportionately affect Black women.

Pay attention to blood pressure and lipid improvements, as cardiovascular disease is a leading cause of mortality.

Practical tips:

Work closely with a healthcare team to set realistic goals.

Monitor blood sugar and blood pressure regularly.

Combine medication with culturally tailored nutrition and physical activity.
