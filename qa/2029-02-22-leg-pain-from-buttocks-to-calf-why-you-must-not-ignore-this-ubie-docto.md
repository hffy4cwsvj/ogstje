Leg pain radiating from the buttocks down to the calf is most commonly caused by sciatica, a condition involving irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve. Less common causes include tumors, blood clots (deep vein thrombosis), or spinal disorders such as a herniated disc or spinal stenosis.

Key points about buttock-to-calf leg pain:

Sciatica is the leading cause, often triggered by a herniated disc, bone spur, or narrowing of the spine pressing on the sciatic nerve.

Symptoms may include sharp, burning, or shooting pain, numbness, tingling, or muscle weakness along the leg.

Serious causes like blood clots or tumors are rare but require prompt medical attention, especially if pain is sudden, severe, or paired with swelling or redness.

Because leg pain can stem from many overlapping conditions—some minor, some serious—identifying the root cause quickly matters.
