Low back pain during exercise is often caused by improper form, overuse, or weak core and back muscles. It's especially common among athletes, runners, and weight lifters, but can affect anyone who trains regularly. Contributing factors may include tight hip flexors, poor posture, insufficient warm-ups, or lifting loads that are too heavy. In some cases, persistent pain may signal an underlying issue such as a muscle strain, disc problem, or nerve irritation that needs closer attention.

Explanation

Low back pain while exercising can be caused by several factors. In weight lifters, improper lifting techniques or overloading can strain the back muscles, leading to chronic pain. Athletes, in general, are at risk due to the repetitive movements and physical demands of their sports.

Here are some common reasons why you might experience low back pain during exercise:

Improper Technique: Using the wrong form, especially in activities like weight lifting, can put extra stress on your lower back.

Overuse: Doing too much exercise without enough rest can lead to overuse injuries.

Weak Core Muscles: A weak core can make it harder for your back to support your body during exercise, leading to pain.

To help prevent low back pain, it's important to use proper techniques, gradually increase exercise intensity, and strengthen your core muscles. If you're experiencing persistent discomfort, you can check your symptoms to better understand what might be causing your back pain and whether you should seek medical attention.
