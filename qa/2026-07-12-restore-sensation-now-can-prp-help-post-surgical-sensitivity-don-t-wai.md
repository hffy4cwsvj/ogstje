Restoring Sensation After Breast Surgery: Can PRP Help?

Undergoing breast surgery—whether augmentation, reduction, lift or reconstruction—can affect nipple and breast skin sensitivity. For many patients, a loss or alteration of nipple sensation is distressing. Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) has emerged as a potential tool to support nerve healing and improve outcomes. Here's what you need to know about PRP for nipple sensitivity, based on current evidence, and how to decide whether it might be right for you.

What Is PRP?

PRP is an autologous (your own) blood product enriched with platelets, which contain growth factors that:

Stimulate cell proliferation

Promote blood vessel formation (angiogenesis)

Support tissue repair and nerve regeneration

In practice, a small volume of your blood is drawn, spun in a centrifuge to concentrate the platelets, then activated and injected into the target area.

How Could PRP Improve Nerve Recovery?

After breast surgery, nerve fibers supplying the nipple can be stretched, compressed or cut. PRP's growth factors—such as platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)—may:

Encourage regrowth of damaged nerve fibers (axonal sprouting)

Enhance local microcirculation for better oxygen and nutrient delivery

Modulate inflammation, creating a more favorable healing environment

By supporting these processes, PRP may speed up or amplify natural nerve regeneration, potentially restoring some or all of your nipple sensitivity.

What Does the Research Say?

Clinical studies specifically on PRP for nipple sensitivity are still emerging. However, evidence from related areas offers insight:

Nerve Injury Models: Animal studies report faster functional recovery after PRP injections into injured peripheral nerves.

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome: Small human trials show PRP can reduce pain and improve nerve conduction.

Diabetic Neuropathy: Early data suggests PRP may lessen symptoms like numbness by promoting microvascular health.

While these findings are promising, rigorous randomized trials on PRP for post-surgical nipple sensation are limited. Patients considering this therapy should understand that its use in this setting is still off-label and evolving.

What to Expect During a PRP Treatment

Consultation

Review your medical history and post-surgical course.

Examine your breasts and document current sensation levels.

Blood Draw & Preparation

A nurse draws 20–60 mL of your blood.

A centrifuge concentrates the platelets into a small plasma volume.

Injection

Local anesthetic minimizes discomfort.

PRP is injected around the nipple–areolar complex and along scars or areas of altered sensation.

The procedure takes about 30–60 minutes.

Benefits and Limitations

Benefits

Autologous product with very low risk of allergic reaction

Minimally invasive, office-based procedure

Potential to accelerate natural recovery

Limitations

Not guaranteed—results vary widely among individuals

Slow nerve regrowth: meaningful changes may take 3–6 months

Limited high-quality studies specific to nipple sensation

Cost may not be covered by insurance

Who Might Be a Good Candidate?

Consider discussing PRP if you:

Have persistent numbness or altered sensation 6–12 months after surgery

Do not have signs of active infection or poor wound healing

Have realistic expectations about partial improvement

Are in good overall health and cleared by your surgeon

Always weigh potential benefits against cost, time commitment and the current state of the evidence.

Potential Risks and Considerations

PRP is generally safe, but you should be aware of:

Minor discomfort at injection sites

Temporary swelling, redness or bruising

Infection risk (very low if performed under sterile conditions)

Lack of standardized protocols—technique varies by clinic

Complementary Strategies

In addition to PRP, you might explore:

Sensory Re-education: Gentle massage, vibration or temperature stimulation exercises to engage nerve pathways.

Physical Therapy: Specialized chest-wall mobilization to reduce scar tightness.

Patience: Nerves regenerate at about 1–3 mm per day; full recovery may take up to 18 months.

Next Steps

Talk to your plastic surgeon or a board-certified physician familiar with regenerative therapies.

Ask about published case reports, ongoing clinical trials or peer-reviewed studies that track nipple sensation outcomes after PRP.

Document your symptoms and their progression to share with your healthcare team.

Final Thoughts

PRP represents a promising, low-risk approach to support nerve healing and potentially enhance nipple sensitivity after breast surgery. While evidence is still growing, many patients report incremental improvements in feeling and comfort. The decision to proceed should be informed by a thorough consultation, realistic expectations and a clear understanding of costs and benefits.

If you're experiencing persistent numbness or other sensory changes, always consult a qualified healthcare professional. And remember, for any symptom that feels serious or life-threatening—such as sudden pain, infection signs or uncontrolled bleeding—seek immediate medical attention and speak to a doctor without delay.
