People with PCOS can experience period-like symptoms — such as cramps, bloating, mood swings, and breast tenderness — without actually bleeding. This happens because PCOS causes hormonal imbalances (particularly involving estrogen, progesterone, and androgens) that disrupt ovulation and prevent the uterine lining from shedding, even though the body still goes through some of the physical changes associated with a menstrual cycle.

Because these symptoms can overlap with other conditions like thyroid disorders, endometriosis, or early pregnancy, it's important to identify what's actually driving them before assuming it's PCOS.
