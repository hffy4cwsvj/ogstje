Explanation

Can't focus or concentrate lately? You're not alone. Slipping attention can stem from everyday stress, but sometimes it signals an underlying medical issue. Here are six health-related causes doctors often rule out—and what you can do about each.

Sleep DisordersPoor or insufficient sleep is one of the most common reasons people "can't focus." When you don't get enough high-quality rest, your brain struggles to process information, recall details and stay alert.

• Insomnia: Trouble falling or staying asleep leaves you tired, foggy and irritable. • Sleep apnea: Brief breathing pauses—often unnoticed—fragment sleep and lower oxygen levels, leading to daytime drowsiness and difficulty concentrating. • Restless legs syndrome: Urge to move your legs disrupts sleep cycles, resulting in poor daytime focus.

What to do

Keep a consistent bedtime routine (wind down 30 minutes before bed, limit screens).

Talk to your doctor about a sleep study if you snore, gasp at night or feel unrefreshed in the morning.

Address underlying causes (stress, caffeine intake, medications).

Thyroid DysfunctionYour thyroid gland regulates metabolism and energy use. Both an underactive (hypothyroidism) and overactive (hyperthyroidism) thyroid can impair focus.

Mood Disorders: Depression & AnxietyPersistent low mood or excessive worry doesn't just affect emotions—it can make you feel mentally "stuck," unable to focus or complete tasks.

• Depression: Slowed thinking, poor memory, indecision and lack of motivation are common. • Anxiety: Racing thoughts, constant worry and an inability to switch off can sap mental energy.

Nutritional DeficienciesYour brain needs certain nutrients to work efficiently. Missing vitamins and minerals can lead to fatigue, forgetfulness and poor concentration.

• Iron deficiency (anemia): Low iron can cause tiredness, dizziness and trouble concentrating. • Vitamin B12 deficiency: Essential for nerve function; lack of B12 can produce brain fog, memory problems and mood changes. • Vitamin D deficiency: Linked to fatigue, low mood and reduced cognitive function.

Get blood tests to check your iron, B12 and vitamin D levels.

Boost intake of leafy greens, lean meats, fish, eggs, dairy and fortified foods—or discuss supplements with your doctor.

A balanced, whole-food diet supports steady energy and mental clarity.

Medications & SubstancesSome prescription drugs and recreational substances can impair focus as a side effect.

• Prescription meds: Antihistamines, sleep aids, certain blood pressure drugs and some antidepressants can cause drowsiness or clouded thinking. • Alcohol and recreational drugs: Even occasional use can disrupt sleep and cognitive performance. • Caffeine overuse: While moderate caffeine can boost alertness, too much can cause jitteriness, anxiety and crashes that hamper concentration.

Review your current medications with your doctor or pharmacist.

Be honest about alcohol or drug use.

If caffeine is a problem, try cutting back gradually rather than quitting cold-turkey.

Chronic Illness & InflammationLong-term health conditions often come with persistent inflammation, fatigue and "brain fog."

• Diabetes: Blood sugar swings—both high and low—can affect alertness and memory. • Autoimmune diseases (e.g., lupus, rheumatoid arthritis): Chronic inflammation and treatment side effects may impair cognitive function. • Post-viral syndromes (including Long COVID): Ongoing fatigue, muscle aches and concentration difficulties are common.

Work with your primary care provider or specialist to optimize treatment of your chronic condition.

Regular exercise, a balanced diet and stress management can help reduce inflammation and improve mental clarity.

Track your symptoms and blood sugar (if diabetic) to identify patterns that worsen focus.

When to Seek HelpIf you've tried lifestyle tweaks—improving sleep, eating well, managing stress—and still can't focus or concentrate, it's time for a thorough medical evaluation. Early diagnosis and treatment often lead to better outcomes.
