Heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia) can result from several underlying causes, including:

Hormonal imbalances — disruptions in estrogen and progesterone that affect the uterine lining

Uterine conditions — such as fibroids, polyps, adenomyosis, or endometriosis

Blood disorders — including clotting conditions like von Willebrand disease

Medications or IUDs — certain treatments can increase menstrual flow

Thyroid or other medical conditions — that influence the menstrual cycle

Identifying the specific cause is essential, as treatment depends on the root issue. A healthcare provider can order tests and recommend targeted care to manage the bleeding effectively.

Explanation

Heavy menstrual bleeding can happen for different reasons. It might be due to problems with hormones, which are chemicals in the body that help control periods. Sometimes, issues with the uterus (like fibroids, polyps, or endometriosis), can cause heavy bleeding.

Another reason could be a blood disorders affecting how blood clots. If blood doesn't clot well, it can lead to more bleeding.
