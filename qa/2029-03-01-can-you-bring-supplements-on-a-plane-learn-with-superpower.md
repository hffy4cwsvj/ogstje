What TSA rules actually say about supplements

TSA has no blanket ban on vitamins, minerals, protein powders, or other dietary supplements. You can bring supplements on a plane in both carry-on and checked bags. The agency categorizes supplements by physical form, solid, powder, or liquid, and applies different rules to each.

Solid supplements: Tablets, capsules, and gummies

Tablets, capsules, softgels, and gummies are treated as solid items with no quantity limits. A bottle of magnesium glycinate capsules and a jar of collagen gummies both pass through X-ray without issue. TSA agents don't count pills or measure bottle sizes for solid supplements. This is the most hassle-free form to travel with.

Powder supplements and the 12-ounce rule

Powdered supplements fall under TSA's powder policy, implemented in 2018 after concerns about concealing prohibited materials in powder form. For domestic flights, each powder container must be 12 ounces (about 350 milliliters) or less to stay in your carry-on. Anything over that limit must go in checked luggage or be placed in a separate bin at security for additional inspection.

For international last-point-of-departure flights to the U.S., the same 12-ounce threshold applies. Larger powder containers may be prohibited from the cabin entirely if they can't be cleared at screening.

Liquid supplements and the 3-1-1 rule

Liquid supplements follow the standard 3-1-1 carry-on rule: each container must hold 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less, and all containers must fit in one quart-sized clear plastic bag. Most dropper bottles of liquid vitamin D or K2-D3 liquid are one ounce or less and fit easily beside travel toiletries. Larger bottles go in checked luggage.

How to travel with supplements in your carry-on

Organization matters more than most people expect. Loose pills rattling in a toiletry bag look ambiguous on an X-ray screen and invite closer inspection. Keep supplements in original labeled containers whenever possible, labels identify contents, list ingredients, and speed up any agent questions.

If you're consolidating to save space, transfer a week's supply of capsules or tablets into a clearly marked pill organizer. Keep a photo of the original labels on your phone in case you need to identify a supplement at security or customs.

Packing powders in your carry-on

Creatine, greens powders, and fiber supplements in containers under 12 ounces can stay in your bag during regular screening. If you're bringing a large tub over 12 ounces, check it or pull it out for a separate bin and allow a few extra minutes. Fine powders, like collagen peptides, get closer scrutiny than coarser powders since they can obscure other items on X-ray.

Checked luggage eliminates all carry-on size restrictions. No powder limits, no liquid rules, you can pack a full-size tub of protein powder, multiple bottle sizes, and large liquid supplements without restriction. For longer trips or bulky supply quantities, this is the right call.

The main risk is damage or a delayed bag. Pack supplements in sealed plastic bags, place bottles toward the center of your suitcase surrounded by clothing, and wrap glass bottles in a towel. For daily essentials like magnesium, keep a few days' supply in your carry-on as backup in case your checked bag is delayed.

International travel and supplement restrictions

TSA rules govern what leaves U.S. airports. Your destination country's customs regulations determine what you can bring in. Most countries allow personal-use quantities of common vitamins and minerals. But specific ingredients can be restricted, controlled, or banned depending on where you're going.

Supplements that may be restricted abroad

Several widely available U.S. supplements face restrictions or prescription requirements in other countries:

DHEA: Over the counter in the U.S., a controlled substance in Canada and several European countries

Melatonin: Tightly regulated in the UK and EU; requires a prescription in some countries

Herbal supplements: Some countries require phytosanitary certificates for botanicals like ashwagandha and berberine

Hormone-support supplements: Pregnenolone and similar steroid hormone precursors vary in legal status internationally

High-dose amino acids: Certain formulations may be flagged as pharmaceutical-adjacent in some jurisdictions

Bring only what you'll use during your trip, a personal-use quantity is typically a few weeks' supply at most. If a supplement contains an ingredient that could be mistaken for a controlled substance, carry a brief letter from your healthcare provider explaining why you take it.

Which supplements are actually worth packing

Not everything in your stack is worth hauling through security on a short trip. Start with supplements that affect how you feel within 24 to 48 hours of missing a dose, sleep support, digestive enzymes, and energy-related nutrients are the non-negotiables for most people.

Supplements with longer action windows can typically tolerate a few missed days without noticeable effect. For a weekend domestic trip, most fat-soluble vitamins and minerals won't require packing.

When consistency matters most

If you're managing a specific health goal, skipping your routine during travel may have noticeable effects on your progress. Someone using berberine for blood sugar support or ashwagandha for cortisol and stress support, a double-blind RCT in Indian Journal of Psychological Medicine (64 adults) found ashwagandha root extract significantly reduced serum cortisol vs. placebo, though direct evidence for jet-lag-specific cortisol disruption is limited, won't want to break their regimen. Specialty formulations are also hard to replace abroad, quality and dosing vary significantly by country.

Know your numbers before you travel

Travel disrupts sleep, stress hormones, and your body's baseline rhythms. Knowing your biomarker levels before you leave gives you a reference point for how well your body handles that disruption, and whether your supplements are actually doing their job.

Checking your vitamin D level before a long trip establishes a baseline; retesting after you return shows whether travel affected your levels. Monitoring cortisol alongside sleep support markers shows whether your routine is helping keep your cortisol levels stable. For metabolic support supplements like berberine, checking fasting glucose after you return shows whether your levels held steady during travel. HbA1c reflects a ~3-month blood glucose average, so it's more useful for tracking consistency across multiple trips than for gauging the impact of a single journey.

Frequent travelers can use biomarker data to identify which supplements are genuinely non-negotiable. If your ferritin stays stable even when you miss a few days of iron supplementation, that's your signal it's not critical to pack. Frequently Asked Questions

Can you take powder supplements on a plane in your carry-on?

Yes. Powder supplements are allowed in carry-on bags as long as each container holds 12 ounces or less. Containers over 12 ounces must go in checked luggage or be placed in a separate bin for additional TSA screening at the security checkpoint.

Do you need to declare supplements at airport security?

You don't need to verbally declare supplements at security. Powders over 12 ounces must come out of your carry-on and go into a separate screening bin. Liquid supplements must follow the 3-1-1 rule and fit in your quart-sized liquids bag alongside other toiletries.

Can you bring vitamins in unmarked containers through TSA?

You can, but original labeled packaging makes the process faster and smoother. Unmarked containers of capsules or powder look ambiguous on X-ray and increase your odds of secondary screening. Labeled bottles or clearly marked travel containers reduce friction at the checkpoint and prevent dosing errors on the road.

Are there restrictions on bringing supplements internationally?

Some countries restrict specific supplement ingredients, DHEA in Canada, melatonin across much of the EU, and certain herbal products in various jurisdictions. Check your destination country's customs rules before traveling, bring only personal-use quantities, and keep everything in labeled containers to avoid issues at customs.

What's the best way to pack supplements for a long flight?

Pack essential daily supplements in your carry-on in original labeled bottles or a marked pill organizer. Place large powder tubs in checked luggage to stay within the 12-ounce carry-on limit. Split your supply between carry-on and checked bags so you're covered if one bag is delayed.

Can TSA confiscate your supplements?

TSA can confiscate supplements that violate carry-on rules, liquid containers over 3.4 ounces or powders over 12 ounces you're unwilling to have screened separately. Supplements packed in compliant containers are rarely confiscated. The more common outcome for oversized items is being asked to check the bag or surrender the item at the checkpoint.

Sleep and stress-support supplements, like L-theanine and magnesium, are especially worth bringing on long flights for exactly this reason.

Split your supply between carry-on (3–5 days of essentials) and your checked bag (full supply, large tubs, glass bottles) to protect against delayed luggage.
