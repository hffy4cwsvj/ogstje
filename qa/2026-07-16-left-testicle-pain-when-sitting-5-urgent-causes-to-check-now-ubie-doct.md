Explanation

Experiencing a sharp pain in left testicle when sitting can be uncomfortable—and sometimes alarming. The good news is that many causes are treatable and not life-threatening. That said, testicular pain should never be ignored, especially if it comes on suddenly or severely.

Below are five of the most common reasons your left testicle may hurt when you sit, what they feel like, and when to seek medical care.

1. Testicular Torsion (A Medical Emergency)

Testicular torsion happens when the spermatic cord twists, cutting off blood flow to the testicle. This is a medical emergency that requires immediate treatment—usually surgery—within hours to prevent permanent damage.

2. Epididymitis (Inflammation or Infection)

Epididymitis is inflammation of the epididymis—the coiled tube behind the testicle that stores sperm. It's one of the most common causes of testicular pain in men and often affects just one side.

Common causes:

Bacterial infections

Sexually transmitted infections (such as chlamydia or gonorrhea)

Urinary tract infections

Gradual onset of pain (not usually sudden)

Tenderness in the back of the testicle

Swelling

Burning with urination

Possible fever

You may notice sharp pain in left testicle when sitting, especially if pressure is applied to the inflamed area. Sitting for long periods can aggravate the discomfort.

Epididymitis is usually treated with antibiotics. Early treatment reduces the risk of complications.

3. Varicocele (Enlarged Veins in the Scrotum)

A varicocele is a group of enlarged veins within the scrotum—similar to varicose veins in the leg. It most commonly occurs on the left side due to differences in vein anatomy.

Why it hurts when sitting:

Sitting can increase pressure in the pelvic and groin veins, which may worsen discomfort.

Dull, aching pain that may become sharp

Heaviness in the scrotum

Pain that worsens after long periods of sitting or standing

Visible or palpable "bag of worms" feeling

While varicoceles are often harmless, they can affect fertility in some men. If the pain persists or worsens, a doctor may recommend imaging tests or treatment options.

4. Inguinal Hernia

An inguinal hernia occurs when part of the intestine pushes through a weak spot in the abdominal wall and into the groin.

Signs to watch for:

Bulge in the groin area

Pain or pressure that worsens when sitting, bending, or lifting

A dragging sensation in the scrotum

Occasional sharp pain in left testicle when sitting

The pain happens because sitting increases abdominal pressure, which pushes more tissue into the hernia space.

Small hernias may only cause mild discomfort. Larger or painful hernias often require surgical repair. If the pain becomes severe or the bulge becomes firm and cannot be pushed back in, seek urgent care.

5. Referred Pain (From Back, Nerves, or Kidneys)

Not all testicle pain starts in the testicle.

The nerves supplying the testicles originate in the lower back and abdomen. Problems in these areas can send pain signals to the scrotum.

Possible sources:

Lower back strain

Herniated disc

Kidney stones

Pinched nerve

If your sharp pain in left testicle when sitting is accompanied by:

Lower back pain

Pain radiating down the leg

Flank pain (side of abdomen)

Blood in urine

Then the cause may be outside the scrotum itself.

Treating the underlying condition usually resolves the testicular discomfort.
